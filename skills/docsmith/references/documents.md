# Documents

Reports, memos, proposals, letters, and other formatted documents (Word, Google Docs, PDF). Read this when building a document, or reviewing one that is long, formal, or will be published. Sources are in the drawer's `research/docsmith.md`, section 3.

## Structure

* Main point first. The first paragraph states the conclusion, decision, or request. Background comes after. Flag a document that builds up to its point.
* A long report has an executive summary that stands alone, leads with the recommendation, and runs about 5 to 15 percent of the length.
* Decision documents are mostly full sentences, not bullet lists.
* Ideas are grouped under one governing point per section, in a clear order.
* Headings are informative: a reader skimming only the headings should learn the content. "Introduction", "General", or "Other" alone are weak; flag them, and leave the wording to unslop. Question headings suit FAQs and reference material; statements suit the rest.
* Building a document: write the headings first and check they read as the argument, then fill sections.

## Headings and navigation

* Headings use real heading styles, never bold or large Normal text. One title (Title style or a single top heading), then levels in order with none skipped.
* At most three heading levels in most documents. Show hierarchy with the fewest cues: space above, then a modest size step, then bold. No underlines, no all caps.
* Long reports: numbered headings (1, 1.1, 1.1.1) applied through the heading styles, a generated contents list, page numbers.
* Headings are set to keep with the next paragraph.
* Essential content never lives only in a header or footer.

## Typography

* Body 10 to 12 pt in print, 15 to 25 px on screen.
* Line spacing about 120 to 145 percent of the font size.
* Lines about 45 to 90 characters. Flag only below about 40 or above about 100; the research on exact length is mixed. Wide one inch margins at 12 pt usually make lines too long.
* One font, two at most. Do not flag a font as wrong unless it is a display, novelty, or monospaced face used for body text.
* Flag: double spaces, underlining that is not a link, all caps longer than a line, bold and italic together, straight quotes in typeset text, double hyphens standing in for punctuation, first line indents combined with space between paragraphs.

## Styles over manual formatting

* All formatting comes from styles: Normal, Title, Heading 1 to 3, List, Caption, Quote, Table.
* No blank paragraphs for spacing; use space before and after. No tabs or spaces for alignment; use tab stops, tables, or indents in the style.
* Lists use real list styles. Numbers only where order or count matters.
* Captions use the caption feature; references to figures and tables are cross reference fields, not typed numbers.
* Page breaks, not runs of empty lines.

## Tables and figures

* Tables have a marked header row that repeats on each page. No merged or split cells in data tables. No tables used only for layout.
* Numbers right aligned, text left aligned (see `charts-and-tables.md`).
* Figures sit inline with the text so reading order holds, with a caption and alt text.

## Accessibility

* Text contrast at least 4.5:1, large text 3:1.
* Colour never the only carrier of meaning.
* Alt text on every meaningful image: what it means, not "image of". Decorative images marked decorative. Complex charts get the data in the text or a table.
* Link text says where it goes; no "click here".
* Document title and language are set in the properties.
* PDF exports are tagged, have a title that is shown in the window, and keep the heading structure. Aim for PDF/UA. A scanned or untagged PDF is a flag.
* Building to WCAG 2.2 AA meets Section 508 and the European Accessibility Act.

## PDFs (review only)

Check: tagged or not, title set, headings present in the tags, reading order, alt text, contrast, selectable text (not a scan). Flag fixes for the source document rather than editing the PDF.

## Common fixes and flags

Fix: fake headings turned into heading styles, skipped heading levels, blank line spacing replaced by paragraph spacing, inconsistent fonts or sizes, header rows marked and repeated, missing alt text you can write from what the image shows, document title and language.

Flag: main point buried, missing executive summary in a long report, structure that needs reorganizing, a document built entirely with manual formatting (needs restyling), an untagged PDF.
